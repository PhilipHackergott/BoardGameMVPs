# Narrative & Story Playbook — use John Truby's *Anatomy of Story*

> **Factory rule (standing, user-set 2026-05-25):** **lay out every game's story with John Truby's
> *The Anatomy of Story* method.** Any project with narrative — the main campaign *and* every side
> story / character arc / quest — gets a **`narrative-design.md`** built on Truby's structure. Don't
> improvise plot ad hoc. Exemplar: `projects/spaeti/narrative-design.md`.

## Why
Truby's structure is organic (grows from character, not a mechanical 3-act grid) and it scales:
the **seven steps** are the minimum any complete story needs; the **twenty-two steps** add the plot
detail of a full campaign; multi-strand games get **one strand per character/quest, each hitting the
seven steps**. This is exactly how a game's quest web should be built.

## The upstream work (do this before steps) — Truby's "premise → designing principle"
1. **Premise** — the whole story in one sentence (a character, the event that starts it, the outcome).
2. **Designing principle** = **story process + original execution** — the synthesizing idea that
   organizes the *whole* story in an original way (often a structural metaphor: a journey, a single
   day, **the four seasons**, a tournament season, a symbol). *"Find it and never take your eye off it."*
3. **Theme / moral argument** — what the story says about how to act; runs as its own seven-step
   values progression (weakness→desire→opposition→plan→self-revelation→moral decision→new equilibrium).
4. **Character web** — define the hero **against** the others (four-corner opposition): the **main
   opponent** competes for the **same goal** and attacks the hero's **greatest weakness**; plus
   ally(s), a **fake-ally-opponent**, and/or a fake-opponent-ally. Never design the hero alone.
5. **Hero's change `W × A = C`** — Weakness (give a **moral** need *and* a psychological need — moral =
   the hero is hurting others) × the **basic Action** → the **Changed** person at self-revelation.
   Give the hero a **ghost** (an unresolved wound from the past).

## The SEVEN KEY STEPS — for every side story / character arc / quest (and the minimum for any story)
1. **Weakness & Need** (psychological + moral) — what's holding them back; what they must learn.
2. **Desire** — a specific goal that gives the line its spine (start it *low*, let it build).
3. **Opponent** — the person competing for the **same goal**, who attacks the weakness.
4. **Plan** — how the hero goes after the desire.
5. **Battle** — the final conflict over the goal.
6. **Self-Revelation** — what the hero learns (psychological + moral); the end-point of the change.
7. **New Equilibrium** — the new normal, at a higher (or lower) level.
> In a game: each NPC arc, side quest, and vignette = **one seven-step strand**. If you can't cover all
> seven, it isn't a complete story and will feel forced — cut it or finish it.

## The TWENTY-TWO STEPS — for the MAIN campaign / central arc
`1 Self-revelation, need & desire (the frame) · 2 Ghost & story world · 3 Weakness & need · 4 Inciting
event · 5 Desire · 6 Ally/allies · 7 Opponent and/or mystery · 8 Fake-ally opponent · 9 First
revelation & decision (changed desire/motive) · 10 Plan · 11 Opponent's plan & main counterattack ·
12 Drive · 13 Attack by ally · 14 Apparent defeat · 15 Second revelation & decision (obsessive drive) ·
16 Audience revelation · 17 Third revelation & decision · 18 Gate/gauntlet/visit to death · 19 Battle ·
20 Self-revelation · 21 Moral decision · 22 New equilibrium.`
- Not a fixed formula — it's the **organic order** of how people solve a life problem; be flexible,
  keep it character-driven. A story may have more/fewer; never fewer than the seven.
- The **reveals sequence** (steps 9/15/17 + audience reveal) should escalate, quicken toward the end,
  and each reveal should **change the hero's desire/motive**.

## How it maps onto a game (the factory convention)
- **`narrative-design.md`** per project: premise · designing principle · theme/moral argument ·
  character web · the hero's `W×A=C` + ghost · the **main arc as the 22 steps** mapped to the game's
  macro-structure (acts/seasons/tournament-ladder) · a **bank of side-story strands, each as the 7
  steps** · how each beat is *delivered in play* (which gameplay event/trigger fires it).
- **Designing principle is often the game's macro-loop:** a season (Späti), a tournament season /
  discover→Worlds career (TCG), a run (roguelike). Let the structure metaphor *be* the progression.
- **Content-depth applies:** side-story strands are **data on the vignette/quest registry** (see
  `content-depth.md`); the writing is the product, so build the registry first and treat the strands
  as the catalog. The MVP ships the main arc + a content-complete set of side strands, not a teaser.
- **Spec/GDD hook:** the spec's narrative section points to `narrative-design.md`; acceptance criteria
  include "every shipped side strand completes its 7 steps" and "the main arc hits its mapped 22 steps."
