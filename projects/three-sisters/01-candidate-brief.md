## Title & identity
- **Name:** Three Sisters
- **Designer:** Ben Pinchback & Matt Riddle _(reported)_
- **Publisher:** 25th Century Games (2021)
- **Year:** 2021

## At a glance
- **Players:** 1–4 (includes a **solo** mode)
- **Play time:** ~45–60 minutes _(unverified — typical for a heavier roll-and-write)_
- **Weight:** medium (a meaty, combo-heavy roll-and-write)
- **BGG rating:** **avg ~7.73**, geek (bayes) rating **~5.72** _(reported, Meeple Mountain detail page;
  confirm in analysis)_. No digital version found.

## The core loop
A **shared dice pool** roll-and-write themed on companion planting (corn, beans, squash). Each round the
active player rolls; then **every player** uses the rolled values to write into their own garden sheet,
advancing several **interlocking areas** — vegetable plots, perennials, fruit, bees, the farmers'
market, and equipment upgrades — where filling one area triggers **combos and bonus actions** in others.
Over ~8 rounds you chain these engines for points; **most points wins**. The solo mode pits you against
a dice-drafting/blocking "opponent."

## Why digital
This is the **heaviest-bookkeeping** title on the shortlist, which is exactly where digitization adds the
most value: the sheet is a web of interdependent tracks where one mark cascades into combos and bonuses.
A digital version **validates every legal write, auto-applies the combo chains and bonuses, tracks all
the side resources, and totals the score** — removing genuine upkeep and rules-lookups while keeping the
satisfying engine-building. A strong **solo mode** makes it a complete single-player product.

## Top risk
**Scope.** The interlocking subsystems are the appeal but also the danger: trim too much and it isn't
Three Sisters; include everything and it's no longer a "smallest playable" MVP. The spec stage must draw
a careful, defensible MVP boundary (likely solo + a subset of areas first).

## Top opportunity
Because it has the **heaviest upkeep**, a clean digital version is disproportionately better than the
paper game — the clearest "the app is just better" pitch of the shortlist — and the built-in solo mode
gives a complete single-player MVP.

## Natural player mode (note, not scored)
**Competitive multiplayer + solo.** Since players write simultaneously from a shared roll, **solo and
async** modes are natural; MVP can ship **solo-first**, with hot-seat/async multiplayer to follow.

## IP first impression (note, not scored)
Current commercial title — **not** public domain. 25th Century Games is a mid-size publisher; licensing
posture unknown. Provisional only; analysis sets `ip.status` and weighs licensing vs. reimplementation.

## Recommendation
**Shortlist (pursue with scope caution).** The highest-value digitization of the three, but also the
biggest build — worth advancing to analysis specifically to test whether a tightly-scoped, solo-first
MVP is viable before committing.
